The Problem

Expensive Exits, Unknown Causes

Calloway Group was spending an estimated $2.1M annually on attrition-related costs — recruiting, onboarding, and lost productivity. The consulting division was hardest hit, with a 31% annual turnover rate among mid-level managers.

HR had exit interviews but no systematic way to identify who was at risk before they started looking. Leadership was split: Finance suspected it was salary compression; Operations blamed overtime; HR thought it was poor management. This project settles the debate with data.

Methodology

How It Was Built

1
Exploratory Analysis (Pandas + Seaborn) Calculated attrition rates across all 35 features. Built the department × job level heatmap to surface the highest-risk pockets. Found overtime alone increased attrition probability by 2.3×.
2
Compensation Gap Analysis Compared monthly income distributions between employees who left vs. stayed, by role and level. Ran a Mann-Whitney U test to confirm the compensation gaps were statistically significant (p < 0.01) in Consulting and Sales.
3
Decision Tree Classifier (Scikit-learn) Trained a decision tree (max depth 4) on 80% of records. Achieved 86% accuracy on the test set. Exported and annotated the tree to show HR leadership the exact decision logic in plain English — no ML knowledge required.
4
3-Slide Executive Brief Problem → Findings → Recommendations. Three policy changes: adjust Consulting Level 2 compensation band, cap overtime requirement, and introduce 90-day manager check-ins for new hires in high-risk departments.
